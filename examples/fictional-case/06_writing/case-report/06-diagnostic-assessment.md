## Diagnostic assessment

### Diagnostic methods

On day 0, full blood count showed a white-cell count of {{R-A0-007}} ×10⁹/L, an eosinophil
count of {{R-A0-008}} ×10⁹/L and {{R-A0-009}} atypical lymphocytes; by day +4 these had risen to
{{R-A0-019}} ×10⁹/L, {{R-A0-020}} ×10⁹/L and {{R-A0-021}} respectively. On day 0, liver
biochemistry showed ALT {{R-A0-010}} U/L, AST {{R-A0-011}} U/L and total bilirubin {{R-A0-012}}
µmol/L; by day +4, the peak values were ALT {{R-A0-022}} U/L, AST {{R-A0-023}} U/L and total
bilirubin {{R-A0-024}} µmol/L. Renal function worsened from a pre-illness creatinine of
{{R-A0-042}} µmol/L to {{R-A0-013}} µmol/L on day 0 and a peak of {{R-A0-025}} µmol/L on day +4.
C-reactive protein was {{R-A0-014}} mg/L on day 0 and {{R-A0-026}} mg/L on day +4. Blood
cultures, HBsAg, anti-HCV,
HIV serology and antinuclear antibody were all negative. HHV-6 PCR on blood was positive, at
{{R-A0-027}} copies/mL. HLA-B*58:01 genotyping was positive. Abdominal ultrasound showed mild
hepatomegaly with a homogeneous parenchyma and normal kidneys. Skin biopsy showed a perivascular
dermatitis with a lymphocytic and eosinophilic infiltrate, without full-thickness epidermal
necrosis.

### Diagnostic challenges

The onset more than three weeks after starting allopurinol, rather than within the first days, made
an ordinary drug rash or an infectious exanthem the initial working diagnoses; the facial oedema,
lymphadenopathy, eosinophilia and multi-organ involvement were the findings that redirected the
work-up toward a severe delayed drug hypersensitivity reaction.

### Diagnostic reasoning

Fever, a widespread maculopapular rash beginning weeks after a new drug, facial oedema,
lymphadenopathy, eosinophilia with atypical lymphocytes, and combined hepatic and renal involvement
met RegiSCAR criteria for definite DRESS, with a RegiSCAR score of {{R-A0-015}} [@kardaun2013].
Differential diagnoses considered and their reasons for exclusion were: Stevens-Johnson
syndrome/toxic epidermal necrolysis (no blistering, no mucosal involvement, a negative Nikolsky
sign, and a biopsy without full-thickness epidermal necrosis); acute generalised exanthematous
pustulosis (no pustules, and a slower onset than typical); a viral exanthem or mononucleosis-like
illness (negative viral and autoimmune serologies apart from the HHV-6 reactivation, which is itself
a recognised feature of DRESS rather than an alternative diagnosis [@xiao2018]); and a spontaneous
flare of chronic kidney disease unrelated to drug exposure (argued against by the clear temporal
relationship to allopurinol and by the skin and haematological findings). The positive HLA-B*58:01
result supported allopurinol specifically as the culprit drug [@goncalo2013].

### Prognostic characteristics

A RegiSCAR score of {{R-A0-015}} indicated definite DRESS. HLA-B*58:01 carriage and a
non-renally-adjusted starting dose are established risk factors for developing allopurinol
hypersensitivity syndrome in the first place [@stamp2012; @bathini2022], and their presence here,
together with the pre-existing chronic kidney disease that could itself be worsened by the
reaction, was taken as a marker of a patient who could plausibly have a severe or protracted
course; this guided the decision for prompt systemic corticosteroid treatment and close monitoring
of renal and hepatic function.

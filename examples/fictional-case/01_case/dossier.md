# Case dossier (de-identified, fictional)

This is a fictional case, constructed to illustrate the case-report workflow. All administrative
details (name, institution, city, record number, calendar dates) have been removed or converted to
relative time; no real patient is described.

## Teaching point

A late-onset severe cutaneous adverse drug reaction (DRESS/DiHS) to a urate-lowering drug, with
eosinophilia, atypical lymphocytosis, liver and kidney involvement, and HHV-6 reactivation, in a
patient with chronic kidney disease in whom the drug was started without renal dose adjustment and
without prior HLA-B*58:01 testing.

## Patient summary

- Male, in his 50s.
- Past history: gout (3 years, 4–5 first-metatarsophalangeal flares per year, self-treated with
  colchicine during flares); hypertension (6 years, on amlodipine 5 mg/day, stable); chronic kidney
  disease stage 3a (baseline creatinine and eGFR below).
- No known drug allergy before this event. Non-smoker, light alcohol use.
- HLA-B*58:01 had not been tested before the urate-lowering drug was started.

## History of present illness (relative time; day 0 = hospital admission)

- **Day −28**: outpatient rheumatology visit for a gout flare. Serum uric acid elevated (value in
  facts.csv). Allopurinol 300 mg/day started without adjustment for chronic kidney disease,
  together with prophylactic colchicine.
- **Day −3** (25th day of allopurinol): fever (38.5–39 °C) and pruritus, with an erythematous
  maculopapular rash starting on the trunk and spreading to the limbs.
- **Day −1**: facial oedema; rash now covering more than half of the body surface area. The patient
  stopped allopurinol on this day.
- **Day 0**: hospital admission.

## Clinical findings at admission (day 0)

- Temperature 39.2 °C, heart rate 104/min, blood pressure 128/76 mmHg.
- Facial oedema; confluent erythematous maculopapular rash over approximately 60% of body surface
  area, no blistering, no mucosal erosion, Nikolsky sign negative.
- Bilateral cervical and inguinal lymphadenopathy, nodes 1.5–2 cm, mildly tender.
- Liver edge palpable 2 cm below the costal margin, no jaundice.

- Abdominal ultrasound: mild hepatomegaly with homogeneous parenchyma; both kidneys normal in size.
- Skin biopsy (day +1): perivascular dermatitis with lymphocytic and eosinophilic infiltrate, no
  full-thickness epidermal necrosis.
- RegiSCAR score: 7 (definite DRESS/DiHS).

## Diagnostic reasoning

The combination of fever, a widespread maculopapular rash beginning more than 2 weeks after starting
a new drug, facial oedema, lymphadenopathy, eosinophilia with atypical lymphocytes, and multi-organ
(hepatic and renal) involvement met RegiSCAR criteria for definite DRESS/DiHS. Differentials
considered: Stevens-Johnson syndrome/toxic epidermal necrolysis (against: no blistering, no mucosal
involvement, Nikolsky negative, biopsy without full-thickness necrosis), acute generalised
exanthematous pustulosis (against: no pustules, slower onset), viral exanthem/mononucleosis-like
illness (against: negative serologies apart from HHV-6 reactivation, which is itself a recognised
feature of DRESS), and a flare of chronic kidney disease unrelated to drug exposure (against: the
temporal relationship to allopurinol and the skin/haematological findings). HLA-B*58:01 positivity
supported the causal link to allopurinol specifically.

## Treatment

- Allopurinol discontinued (the patient had already stopped it on day −1); colchicine also stopped.
- Intravenous methylprednisolone 1 mg/kg/day (70 mg/day for a 70 kg patient) from day 0 to day +6,
  then oral prednisolone 60 mg/day with a taper over 8 weeks (completed day +62).
- Antihistamines, topical corticosteroid cream, intravenous fluid support.
- Nephrology consulted; dialysis not required.

## Outcome and follow-up

- Fever resolved by day +3. Rash gradually faded, desquamation from day +7, fully resolved by
  day +17.
- ALT peaked at 486 U/L on day +4, then declined.
- Discharged day +18.
- Follow-up at day +43: no rash recurrence, liver enzymes normal, creatinine 124 µmol/L (close to
  pre-illness baseline).
- Follow-up at day +92 (after completing the corticosteroid taper): no relapse; thyroid function
  normal; allopurinol not restarted. Gout managed with lifestyle measures and colchicine for flares;
  rheumatology to consider an alternative urate-lowering drug after allergy consultation.
- The culprit drug was recorded in the patient's allergy record.

## Patient perspective

The patient described the rash and fever as frightening because they appeared weeks after starting
a medicine he had expected to tolerate well, and he was relieved that stopping the drug and starting
treatment led to full recovery of the skin, liver and kidney findings. He agreed that the drug
should not be used again and wanted the experience written up so that other patients starting the
same drug for gout could be monitored for a reaction that can appear later than typical drug rashes.
